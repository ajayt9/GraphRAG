Meridian Dynamics is one of the largest energy conglomerates in Europe, with headquarters in Geneva and operations spanning fusion research, grid infrastructure, and carbon markets. The company is led by Elena Voss, who became Chief Executive Officer five years ago after the sudden retirement of the previous chairman. Under Voss, Meridian Dynamics quietly launched Project Chimera, an ambitious effort to commercialize compact fusion reactors before any competitor could reach the market.

Project Chimera is managed out of a remote facility known as the Black Harbor Facility, located on the Norwegian coast. Meridian Dynamics contracted the Obsidian Group, a private security and intelligence firm, to protect the site and to vet every researcher who joined the program. Viktor Kessler, a former intelligence officer who now leads special operations for the Obsidian Group, personally oversees security at Black Harbor Facility.

Elena Voss has publicly described Project Chimera as essential to the goals of The Accord, the international climate treaty that Meridian Dynamics helped negotiate in Geneva. Critics, however, have questioned why a supposedly public-interest project is being run with the secrecy of a defense program.
