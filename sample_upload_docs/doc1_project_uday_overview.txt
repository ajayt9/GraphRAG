Project Uday began four years ago as a large-scale solar and battery-storage mission led by Suryodaya Energy Group, a renewable power company headquartered in Ahmedabad, Gujarat. The initiative was founded by Dr. Ananya Rao, a materials scientist who left a research post in Bengaluru to pursue grid-scale storage technology in the Rann of Kutch.

Suryodaya Energy Group proposed building the country's largest battery park on the salt flats of Kutch, a plan that drew both government backing and private investment from across India. The scale of Project Uday made it the flagship renewable initiative for the entire western region.

Oversight of the project fell to the Central Renewable Council, a national regulatory body headquartered in Delhi. The Council certified the initial engineering design and ordered periodic safety audits, arguing that a project of this scale near ecologically sensitive wetlands required continuous scrutiny.

Vikram Sinha, the chief operating officer of Suryodaya Energy Group, led day-to-day operations and reported directly to the board in Mumbai. Priya Nair joined as legal counsel shortly after the Central Renewable Council granted initial certification, tasked with navigating the regulatory approvals required for a project spanning multiple districts of Gujarat.

By the third year, Project Uday had expanded to four battery installations across the Kutch region, each monitored by sensors feeding data back to Suryodaya Energy Group headquarters in Ahmedabad. The Central Renewable Council continued to certify each phase, though internal memos later suggested some reviewers had reservations about the pace of expansion.
