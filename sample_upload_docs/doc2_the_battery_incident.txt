The Battery Incident occurred midway through the third year of Project Uday, when a battery module at one of the storage sites overheated and released chemical fumes across a nearby salt-farming settlement. Suryodaya Energy Group initially described the release as minor, but internal logs later obtained by journalists told a different story.

Konkan Consortium, an alliance of environmental and fisheries groups along the western coast, first flagged irregularities in the public safety data. Konkan Consortium had been monitoring Project Uday since its founding and had repeatedly requested access to raw sensor logs, requests that Suryodaya Energy Group declined citing proprietary concerns.

Deccan Wire Press, a regional news outlet based in Bengaluru, began investigating after receiving an anonymous tip about The Battery Incident. Reporters traced maintenance records and shipment manifests, building a timeline that contradicted the official account issued by Suryodaya Energy Group.

Dr. Ananya Rao, still employed by Suryodaya Energy Group at the time, grew increasingly uneasy about the discrepancies. She had worked closely with Vikram Sinha during the project's founding and was familiar with the internal safety protocols that appeared to have been bypassed before the incident.

The Central Renewable Council, informed of the incident by Konkan Consortium, ordered an emergency review of all active battery sites. Suryodaya Energy Group was required to suspend operations pending the outcome, the first formal regulatory action taken against the project since Project Uday began.
